Judges decide criminal charges, evictions, family disputes and appeals, yet most judicial races get little attention, and many judges run without an opponent. “Unopposed” means only one name is on the ballot; the judge still needs at least one vote, and you may write in a different name. A bar rating is an evaluation of a candidate by a lawyers’ association (for example the King County Bar Association) that reviews qualifications; it is an opinion of that group, not an official finding.

How to read this page. It lists only public professional information, and each fact has a numbered source link. Statements written by candidates are labeled as such and are not fact-checked by the state or county. Endorsements are advocacy and appear in a separate section for each race. Where a fact could not be found, it says “Not found” with the date checked. This page does not tell you how to vote.

How to research a judge yourself

  • Superior Court cases (KC Script). The King County Superior Court Clerk’s KC Script Portal lets you search for a case number without an account; viewing or downloading documents requires an account. See the Clerk’s records access page.
  • District Court and other state courts. The Washington Courts Odyssey Portal and the fee-based JIS-Link cover district, municipal and superior court records.
  • Seattle Municipal Court. Start at the court’s public records page and its case portal.
  • Written opinions. Search courts.wa.gov/opinions for Supreme Court and Court of Appeals slip opinions. The opening line of an opinion names its author.
  • Judicial discipline. The Commission on Judicial Conduct lets you search public actions by name (leave first name blank to avoid spelling mismatches).
  • Lawyer discipline and admission year. The WSBA Legal Directory shows each lawyer’s admission date and public discipline.
  • Your own ballot. Confirm which races you will see in VoteWA, and read the full Voters’ Guide.
